# Writing a brief

Everything you write here is read by a busy person immediately before they act on it —
a bid/no-bid call, an outreach email, a capture decision. Two or three sentences, then
the structured fields.

## The shape

> Nexagen Solutions does cyber engineering and IT modernisation for DoD customers.
> SDVOSB, roughly 200 staff, holds a CIO-SP3 vehicle. Plausible small-business teaming
> partner on the RS3 task order.

Facts first, then the read. Third person, present tense, the subject at the front. Only
what a source states — a capability you cannot see on a page did not happen, and a
number you are unsure of is left out rather than approximated.

For an opportunity rationale, the same shape carries: the fit read, the specific gates
to confirm, the winnability read, the recommendation.

## No adjectives about people or companies

No "seasoned", no "well-regarded", no "leading provider", no "passionate about". If you
find yourself writing an adjective about somebody rather than a fact about their work,
delete the sentence.

**The test:** could a rep repeat this sentence to the person on a call without
embarrassment?

- Fine: *"You've held the CIO-SP3 vehicle since 2022."*
- Not fine: *"You're a well-established industry leader."*

## Say what is unverified

If a claim is not something you read, mark it. `unverified` and `to confirm` are useful
words that cost you nothing. A rationale that says "confirm RS3 IDIQ access and SECRET
FCL" is doing its job; one that quietly assumes both is setting up a wasted week.

## Length

Two or three sentences. If the only thing you can say is what the record already shows
on screen, **write nothing**. An empty panel costs a rep nothing; a paragraph that
restates a field they can already see costs them the time it takes to work that out.

There is no way to say nothing at length that is worth the reading.
