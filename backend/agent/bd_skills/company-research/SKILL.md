# Researching a company

The question is never "what is this company called". It is:

> **Could they help us win work, take work from us, or award us work?**

Everything below serves that.

## Where to look, in order

1. **Their own site** — `/about`, `/capabilities`, `/services`, `/contract-vehicles`.
   The company describing its own business is the authoritative source for what it
   does, and it is the only source strong enough to be written to a record on its own.
2. **SAM.gov** — a registered entity gives you NAICS codes, size, socio-economic
   status and a UEI. For govcon this outranks any marketing page.
3. **LinkedIn** — headcount and how they describe themselves to hiring candidates.
4. **Reputable directories and press** — last resort, and only with the URL.

Stop as soon as you can answer the question. You are on a budget, and a fast "I could
not find them" is worth more than a slow invention.

## What actually matters, in order

- **Capability** — what they build, operate or deliver. In concrete terms.
- **Customer set** — DoD? civilian agencies? commercial only? A purely commercial firm
  is usually irrelevant to a federal pursuit, however impressive.
- **Size and status** — small business, 8(a), SDVOSB, WOSB, HUBZone. This decides
  whether they are a set-aside teaming route or a large-business subcontractor.
- **Vehicles** — do they hold an IDIQ, GWAC or BPA relevant to this work?
- **Relationship** — partner, competitor, incumbent, or the customer themselves.

## The trap

Do not infer capability from the company's **name**. "Summit Technical Staffing" may
not be a staffing firm. "Redstone Cyber" may do logistics. A name is marketing.

Do not infer capability from the **domain** either. `nexagen-solutions.com` tells you
nothing except that someone owns that domain.

Read a page that says what they do, or report that you could not.

## Write the answer for a BD rep

Two clauses, concrete, no adjectives about how impressive they are.

- Good: `Cyber engineering and IT modernisation for DoD; SDVOSB, ~200 staff.`
- Bad: `A leading provider of innovative technology solutions.`

The second sentence tells a rep nothing they did not already assume, and costs them the
time it takes to find that out.

## When you cannot find them

Say so, plainly, and stop. A parked domain, a personal site, a mail provider, or a firm
with no web presence are all real answers. A miss must stay a miss: a wrong industry
quietly mis-ranks that company for every bid it is ever considered for, and nobody will
know to check it.
